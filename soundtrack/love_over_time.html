Chapter 1

Discovering Love

Lightness, imagination, and the first recognition that romantic feeling has begun.

Track 01

Reality

Richard Sanderson
Life-course stageAdolescence · first romantic attraction
Relationship theme

The first moment when romantic attention feels different from ordinary friendship.

Course concept

Adolescent romantic initiation and the developmental role of crushes.

Course connection

Lecture 14 explains that adolescence is a time when attachment begins to expand beyond family and friendship. Romantic relationships give young people a space to explore identity, learn social expectations, and practice new roles. “Reality” captures that shift through the feeling of a crush becoming emotionally real.

The title suggests a crush becoming emotionally real. This track begins the soundtrack before intimacy and commitment have fully formed.

Track 02

Golden Hour

JVKE
Life-course stageEarly romantic love · idealization
Relationship theme

The relationship feels luminous, extraordinary, and emotionally intense.

Course concept

Romanticized beliefs versus growth beliefs.

Course connection

Lecture 7 distinguishes romanticized beliefs from growth beliefs. “Golden Hour” stays close to the romanticized side of early love, when a new partner can make everything feel effortless and extraordinary. I use it here to show both the pleasure of idealization and the contrast that later relationships will create.

The title turns early love into a special period of light. It captures intensity while reminding us that this is a stage, not the permanent condition of a relationship.

Track 03

Always Online

JJ Lin
Life-course stageEmerging adulthood · long-distance connection
Relationship theme

Staying connected when physical proximity is limited.

Course concept

Online relationship maintenance and technology-mediated intimacy.

Course connection

Lecture 9 shows how technology can support relationship maintenance through self-disclosure, emotional support, positivity, and reassurance. It also points out that digital contact can create distraction and uncertainty. “Always Online” holds both ideas together because being reachable can feel like care, but presence still depends on how people communicate.

The title makes reachability a symbol of care. Being available becomes one way partners communicate presence across distance.

Chapter 2

Falling in Love

Growing intimacy, reachability, care, and the joy of being chosen.

Track 04

Heartbeat

Leehom Wang
Life-course stageEmerging adulthood · relationship strain
Relationship theme

Emotion remains strong while partners struggle to communicate and move forward.

Course concept

Conflict escalation and negative affect reciprocity.

Course connection

Lecture 17 describes how conflict can escalate when hurt is answered with more hurt. “Heartbeat” fits this moment because the relationship still carries strong emotion, but that intensity does not solve the problem. It shows why partners need communication, emotional regulation, and repair.

The title uses a bodily signal to suggest anxiety and uncertainty. Love does not prevent conflict; conflict reveals whether partners can respond constructively.

Track 05

Appearing and Leaving

Liang Bo
Life-course stageEmerging adulthood · relationship dissolution
Relationship theme

A relationship ends, but attachment, memories, and routines do not disappear immediately.

Course concept

Breakup experience and post-dissolution adjustment.

Course connection

Lecture 19 presents breakup as a transition that can disrupt routines, roles, and imagined futures. “Appearing and Leaving” gives that transition a clear shape. Someone can leave the relationship while memories and habits remain, so recovery becomes a process of reflection and support.

The title emphasizes movement between presence and absence. It presents dissolution as a process rather than one final moment.

Interlude · Track 06

What is love?

The Answer · Yang Kun and Guo Caijie

After attraction, passion, conflict, and loss, the project turns from how love feels to what love requires.

Track 06

The Answer

Yang Kun and Guo Caijie
Life-course stageReflection · reconsidering romantic beliefs
Relationship theme

Reconsidering what love means after relational experience.

Course concept

Growth beliefs and the intimacy, passion, and commitment framework.

Course connection

Lecture 7 contrasts the belief that love should solve everything with the more realistic idea that relationships develop through effort. Lecture 11’s Triangular Theory of Love also separates intimacy, passion, and commitment. “The Answer” brings these ideas together by turning love into a question that has to be understood through experience.

The title becomes the project’s turning point. Love is no longer only an intensity; it becomes a question about growth, meaning, and responsibility.

Chapter 3

Uncertainty & Growth

Conflict, loss, reflection, and the work of becoming more realistic about love.

Track 07

Make You Feel My Love

Adele
Life-course stageInto adulthood · mature romantic love
Relationship theme

Love becomes visible through support, reassurance, and responsiveness.

Course concept

Intimacy and relationship-enhancement maintenance.

Course connection

Lecture 11 describes intimacy through warmth, understanding, trust, and support. Lecture 13 adds that relationships are maintained through positivity, openness, and reassurance. Adele’s “Make You Feel My Love” connects to both lectures because it presents care as something a partner actively communicates.

The title is organized around an action directed toward another person. Love is not only felt internally; it is communicated through dependable care.

Track 08

I Do

Karen Mok
Life-course stageInto adulthood · long-term partnership
Relationship theme

Choosing a shared future and making commitment explicit.

Course concept

Long-term orientation and personal, structural, and moral commitment.

Course connection

Lecture 12 describes commitment as a long term orientation and distinguishes personal commitment, structural commitment, and moral commitment. “I Do” focuses on the personal choice to build a future together, while its title also suggests the social ritual that makes that choice visible.

The title is a short statement and a decision. It represents commitment as an intentional transition, not merely proof that a relationship feels passionate.

Track 09

Slowly Falling in Love with You

Karen Mok
Life-course stageEstablished long-term relationship · everyday love
Relationship theme

Long-term love is renewed through ordinary routines and repeated care.

Course concept

Relationship maintenance and companionate love.

Course connection

Lecture 13 describes maintenance as the everyday work that keeps a relationship satisfying and helps repair it after strain. The song’s slow pace reflects that idea. Love is renewed through ordinary conversations, shared routines, openness, and small acts of reassurance.

The word “slowly” makes love cumulative. Long-term connection is built through ordinary days rather than only through dramatic declarations.

Track 10

Time in a Bottle

Jim Croce
Life-course stageLater adulthood · enduring companionship
Relationship theme

Shared time, mortality, memory, and emotionally meaningful companionship.

Course concept

Socioemotional selectivity and relationships in late adulthood.

Course connection

Lecture 14 connects later adulthood with changing priorities around time, health, caregiving, loss, and mortality. “Time in a Bottle” makes those ideas personal by treating shared time as precious. At this point in the soundtrack, love is expressed through the history two people have built together.

The title makes time feel finite. At the end of the soundtrack, love is measured less by intensity than by shared history and the value of the time two people have together.

Chapter 4

Understanding Love

Commitment, maintenance, shared time, and the meaning of enduring companionship.

Final track · Final thought

At first, love feels like a moment.

Over time, it becomes a relationship, a choice, a practice, and shared history.

Across the life course, romantic love may shift from attraction and emotional intensity toward care, commitment, maintenance, and enduring companionship.
